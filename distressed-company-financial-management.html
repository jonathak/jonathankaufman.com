Distressed Company Financial Management

A company facing financial distress may need more frequent cash reporting, careful control of disbursements, and financial information that can support negotiations with lenders, creditors, buyers, and other stakeholders.

The responsibilities can change significantly when a company is preparing for Chapter 11 or operating as a debtor in possession. Financial management must remain connected to day-to-day operations while accommodating court requirements and the work of legal and restructuring advisors.

Financial Management in Distress

Short-Term Cash Forecasting

Prepare and maintain weekly cash forecasts, monitor collections and essential disbursements, and identify near-term funding gaps.

Liquidity & Payment Priorities

Evaluate which expenditures are needed to maintain operations and how timing, contractual commitments, and available cash affect payment decisions.

Financial Reporting & Records

Keep accounting records current, reconcile financial information, and prepare schedules needed by management, lenders, and professional advisors.

Creditor & Claims Analysis

Organize obligations and creditor information, reconcile balances, and support analysis of claims, priorities, and potential recoveries.

Asset Sales & Financing Alternatives

Prepare financial information for prospective buyers or lenders and compare the cash and operating implications of alternative transactions.

Coordination with Advisors

Work with bankruptcy counsel, restructuring advisors, accountants, and other professionals while maintaining the financial information needed to operate the business.

Preparing for or Operating in Chapter 11

Pre-Filing Financial Preparation

Assemble cash forecasts, debt and creditor schedules, financial statements, and transaction records for counsel and restructuring advisors.

Debtor-in-Possession Reporting

Coordinate the financial data needed for monthly operating reports, cash budgets, and other reporting obligations applicable to the case.

Plan & Recovery Analysis

Model potential sale proceeds, administrative costs, creditor recoveries, and the funding implications of proposed restructuring plans.

Questions in Practice

How long can the business continue operating?

A near-term cash forecast can clarify the timing of a funding shortfall and the practical choices available before that point.

What financial information will advisors and creditors need?

The answer depends on the company's debt structure, operating situation, and proposed path, but accurate cash records and reconciled obligations are central.

How can management keep the business operating during a case?

Financial oversight must accommodate essential operating needs, authorized spending, reporting deadlines, and coordination with counsel on applicable restrictions.
