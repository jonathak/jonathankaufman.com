Startup Financial Management

Early-stage companies need financial information that can support decisions about hiring, development, financing, and the use of limited cash. The work changes as a company moves from initial funding to a more established operating business.

Financial management at this stage often combines day-to-day oversight with planning for the next financing. A company may need experienced financial leadership before it needs, or can justify, a full-time finance executive.

Financial Management

Cash Management & Runway

Maintain a current view of cash balances, expected receipts and payments, and the funding needed to reach upcoming milestones.

Budgets & Forecasts

Build and update operating budgets, hiring plans, and financial projections as development schedules and business assumptions change.

Financial Reporting

Prepare financial information for management, directors, and investors, and coordinate with external accountants on bookkeeping, financial statements, tax filings, and audits.

Capitalization & Equity

Maintain capitalization records, evaluate option grants and financing dilution, and coordinate valuations and securities documentation with appropriate outside professionals.

Financing Preparation

Develop financial projections and use-of-proceeds schedules, organize financial due diligence, and evaluate proposed financing terms.

Financial Operations

Coordinate banking, payments, payroll, insurance, vendor arrangements, and outside accounting and legal resources without unnecessarily expanding the internal organization.

Questions in Practice

How much capital should the company raise?

The answer depends on expected expenditures, financing lead time, development milestones, and a reasonable margin for delay or unexpected costs.

What should the CEO and board see each month?

Useful reporting connects cash, spending, commitments, and progress against the operating plan rather than presenting financial statements alone.

When is a full-time CFO necessary?

The right arrangement depends on transaction volume, financing activity, reporting requirements, internal staff, and how much ongoing financial judgment the CEO needs.
