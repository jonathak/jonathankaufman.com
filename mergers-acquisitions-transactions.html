Mergers, Acquisitions & Transactions

Corporate transactions can transfer ownership, combine businesses, or move particular assets and obligations from one party to another. The financial outcome depends not only on the price but also on what is being acquired, how the consideration is paid, and which risks remain with each party.

Evaluating a transaction requires connecting its commercial purpose with its financial, contractual, and operational consequences. Two proposals with similar headline values can produce very different outcomes for buyers, sellers, and other stakeholders.

Methods & Concepts

Transaction Structures

Acquisitions may be structured as stock purchases, asset purchases, mergers, or other arrangements. The choice affects which assets and liabilities transfer, the approvals required, tax considerations, and the continuity of contracts and operations.

Transaction Valuation & Deal Economics

Negotiated consideration reflects expectations about future performance, potential synergies, financing needs, and risk. Enterprise value, equity value, assumed debt, cash, and working-capital adjustments must be distinguished when comparing transaction proposals.

Financial & Commercial Due Diligence

Due diligence tests the assumptions underlying a transaction. It may examine financial statements, cash flows, customer and supplier relationships, contractual commitments, intellectual property, regulatory matters, and liabilities that could affect the economics of the deal.

Purchase Agreements & Risk Allocation

Transaction agreements define what is being transferred, the consideration, closing conditions, representations and warranties, indemnification arrangements, and post-closing obligations. These terms determine how identified and unexpected risks are allocated between the parties.

Contingent & Deferred Consideration

Earnouts, milestone payments, seller notes, and other deferred arrangements can bridge differences in expectations or financing capacity. Their value depends on payment conditions, timing, credit risk, and the parties' ability to influence whether the conditions are met.

Closing & Post-Closing Considerations

Closing may require third-party consents, financing, regulatory approvals, or the satisfaction of other conditions. After closing, integration costs, transitional services, working-capital settlements, and continuing obligations can materially affect the transaction's realized results.

Questions in Practice

What exactly is the buyer acquiring?

The answer may include an operating company, selected assets, intellectual property, contracts, or a combination of these. Identifying what transfers—and what does not—is fundamental to evaluating the price and risks.

How does the stated purchase price translate into proceeds?

Debt repayment, transaction expenses, escrow arrangements, working-capital adjustments, taxes, and contingent payments can cause the amount ultimately received by sellers to differ from the headline purchase price.

Which findings from due diligence should change the deal?

New information may warrant a price adjustment, a contractual protection, a change in structure, or a decision not to proceed. The appropriate response depends on the significance and manageability of the risk.

When does contingent consideration make sense?

Milestone-based payments can align consideration with future results, particularly when value depends on uncertain technical or commercial achievements. They can also create disputes over measurement, control, and payment obligations.

How should competing transaction proposals be compared?

A useful comparison considers certainty of closing, timing and form of payment, assumed liabilities, conditions, contingent consideration, and the operational implications of each proposal—not price alone.
