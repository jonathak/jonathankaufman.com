Capital Structure & Securities

A company's capital structure describes how its assets and operations are financed and how economic claims are distributed among investors and creditors. Different securities may carry different rights to payments, conversion, control, and proceeds from a sale or liquidation.

Understanding these rights is essential to analyzing financing alternatives, ownership dilution, and the value of individual securities. The economic consequences of a transaction may differ substantially from what headline valuations or ownership percentages suggest.

Methods & Concepts

Common & Preferred Equity

Common and preferred shares can differ in voting rights, dividend provisions, liquidation preferences, and participation in proceeds. These distinctions affect how value is distributed among shareholders under different outcomes.

Debt & Convertible Securities

Debt instruments establish contractual claims for principal and interest, often with covenants, maturity provisions, and priorities relative to other obligations. Convertible notes and similar instruments combine debt-like claims with rights to participate in equity value under specified conditions.

Capitalization & Dilution

Capitalization tables track ownership and potential ownership across classes of securities. Financing rounds, option pools, conversions, and warrant exercises can change both the number of shares outstanding and the allocation of economic interests.

Warrants & Stock Options

Warrants and options provide rights, but generally not obligations, to acquire shares under specified terms. Their values depend on the underlying equity, exercise price, remaining term, volatility, and contractual features.

Allocation of Value Among Securities

When a company has multiple classes of securities, enterprise or equity value cannot necessarily be allocated in proportion to stated ownership. Contractual priorities and contingent payoffs may require scenario analysis or option-based valuation methods.

Questions in Practice

How much ownership will a financing dilute?

The answer depends on the pre-money and post-money definitions, the securities issued, conversion provisions, and whether an option pool is expanded before or after the investment.

Who receives the proceeds from a sale?

Liquidation preferences, senior claims, participation rights, and conversion choices determine how proceeds are distributed. A shareholder's percentage ownership may not equal that shareholder's percentage of sale proceeds.

How do convertible instruments affect the capitalization table?

Conversion prices, valuation caps, discounts, accrued interest, and the timing of a financing can change the number and class of shares issued when an instrument converts.

Why can preferred and common shares have different values?

Investors may pay for preferred shares with rights that common shares do not possess. Valuing common stock therefore requires examining the full capital structure rather than simply applying the latest preferred share price.

How should financing alternatives be compared?

Interest costs, repayment obligations, ownership dilution, downside protection, and future financing flexibility can matter as much as the amount of capital raised.
