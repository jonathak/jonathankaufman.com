Biotechnology Asset Assessment, Preservation & Disposition

I provide specialized scientific, clinical, regulatory, intellectual property, and operational support in the assessment, preservation, and disposition of distressed biotechnology assets. My work focuses on identifying issues that may impair asset value, addressing technical and regulatory requirements, and making assets easier for prospective buyers to evaluate and acquire.

I have personally performed this work in connection with the preservation, due diligence, sale, and transfer of biotechnology assets during a Chapter 11 restructuring.

Biotechnology assets can require attention long after a company has reduced its staff. Patent prosecution deadlines, IND reporting obligations, clinical materials, manufacturing records, and specialized vendor arrangements may all affect whether an asset remains useful and transferable. Identifying and addressing these requirements early can prevent avoidable losses, make due diligence more efficient, and help prospective buyers evaluate the assets with greater confidence.

  • Biotechnology asset assessment. Review therapeutic programs, clinical results, patents, INDs, FDA correspondence, CMC information, and other technical assets. Provide the advisory team with a clear inventory of what exists, its development status, material gaps, and issues likely to affect buyer interest or sale value.
  • Patent and regulatory continuity. Identify patent prosecution dates, IND annual reports, regulatory correspondence, and other time-sensitive requirements; develop a practical schedule of actions and preservation costs; and coordinate necessary work with patent counsel and regulatory specialists. This helps avoid preventable lapses and enables the advisory team to budget for essential obligations.
  • Technical due diligence and buyer support. Organize and interpret clinical study reports, datasets, FDA communications, CMC records, patent materials, and development histories; help structure the technical portions of data rooms; and prepare responses to scientific and regulatory questions. Clearer documentation and timely answers can reduce buyer uncertainty, limit transaction delays, and keep prospective purchasers engaged.
  • Biotechnology asset preservation and transfer. Identify and coordinate essential storage, clinical materials, regulatory publishing, database access, manufacturing and other specialized vendor services during a wind-down. Support technical transfer packages, IND sponsorship changes, and handover of development records. Preserving what a buyer needs while discontinuing unnecessary services helps control costs without impairing the assets being sold.
