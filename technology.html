Technology

Emerging-technology companies often begin with a scientific result, a new computational method, or an engineering advance. Turning that work into a business requires understanding what the technology can do, what remains uncertain, and what it will take to develop a product that customers can use.

The areas below involve different scientific disciplines and commercial markets, but they share practical questions about technical feasibility, intellectual property, development milestones, capital requirements, and the path from research to revenue.

Topics

Computational Biology

Computational methods for studying biological systems, including molecular modeling, structural biology, genomics, and the analysis of complex experimental data.

Artificial Intelligence in Drug Discovery

Machine learning applied to target identification, molecular design, screening, and the prediction of biological activity. Commercial applications depend on data quality, experimental validation, and integration into drug development.

Quantum Computing Startups

Companies developing quantum processors, control electronics, error correction, and algorithms. Their financing requirements and commercial prospects vary substantially across hardware architectures and business models.

Therapeutics

Development of new medicines from preclinical research through clinical trials, regulatory review, manufacturing, and commercialization. Financing and company value are closely tied to development milestones and clinical evidence.

Medical Devices

Technologies involving medical instrumentation, diagnostics hardware, and therapeutic devices, with attention to engineering, clinical utility, regulatory pathways, manufacturing, and adoption.

Artificial Intelligence in Diagnostics

Use of machine learning to interpret medical images, laboratory results, and other clinical data. Questions include clinical validation, regulatory clearance, workflow integration, and reimbursement.

Questions in Practice

What has actually been demonstrated?

A laboratory result, a prototype, and a commercially deployable product provide different levels of evidence. The remaining work can determine both the development schedule and the financing required.

Who will use or buy the technology?

The customer may be a pharmaceutical company, hospital, research laboratory, equipment manufacturer, or another technology developer. Each market has different purchasing decisions and requirements for evidence.

What must happen before the next financing?

A new dataset, prototype, clinical result, regulatory interaction, or commercial agreement may materially change a company's prospects. Financial planning should reflect the cost and uncertainty of reaching that milestone.

Where does the company's advantage come from?

Patents, proprietary data, technical know-how, manufacturing processes, and relationships with customers or collaborators can contribute to defensibility, but their importance depends on the business.
